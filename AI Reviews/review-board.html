AIs the retry trap blocking for the prototype, or only for Gate 2?

systems-designeradversarial-qaplayer-psychologist

BLOCKING now. The Gate 1 slice must reach both outcomes, and a Gate 1 acceptance criterion that depends on a Gate 3 system means the prototype can't pass its own gate. Playtesters can't judge fairness with no retry model.

Does Gate 1 accept "restart the slice" as its retry model, or must the final retry and save model be settled first?

BWhat should drive storm escalation?

systems-designerfeasibility-leadplayer-psychologist

Escalate by phase and route position only, or cap how much elapsed time drives it. Otherwise the loop punishes struggling players twice and the deadline can't be tuned offline.

vs

Position-only weather breaks Pillar 2, "The storm sets the clock". Split the channel instead: the readable storm (sound, lightning, wind, ship lights) follows elapsed time, and hazard density follows phase and position.

Escalated decision

Does elapsed time drive anything the player collides with, or only what the player perceives?

CShould the return crossing branch into a riskier shortcut?

player-psychologistsystems-designer

Yes. It gives the pressure an outlet without a second control, gives repairs a mechanical output, gives the dead "unlocked routes" field a purpose, and adds replay value.

vs
feasibility-leadadversarial-qabusiness-analyst

No branching in the prototype, because the forecast is built on one safe-route signal and week 4 is already overloaded. At fixed speed a survivable shortcut is always optimal and doubles the tuning load. Defer it to Gate 2, or cut the unused save field.

Escalated decision

Commit to route choice, with a forecast system built for multiple routes from day one, or remove "unlocked routes" from the save contract?

Escalated decision

Protect the atmosphere by moving discoveries into the untimed act, or protect the pacing by keeping them in the timed act?

Full Board

Round 1 findings · Round 2 cross-examination

systems-designer

Core loop, progression, pacing, difficulty, and the deadline/penalty economy.

Round 1 findings

1
Blocking
What happens after failure is undefined, and saving remaining time at checkpoints can produce unwinnable saves.

A checkpoint reached after several collisions locks in a time budget too small to finish, so a checkpoint retry drops the player into a run they can't win. This contradicts Gate 2's "no dead-end save". Checkpoints are scheduled for Gate 3, but the fail state has to work at Gate 1.

Round 2: Core stays BLOCKING. The "first boarding" sub-point was withdrawn as subsumed by adversarial-qa #3.

2
Major
Weather escalating with elapsed deadline time creates a death spiral.

Collisions consume time, lost time advances the weather, and denser hazards and lower visibility cause more collisions. The system punishes struggling players twice and leaves skilled players under less pressure.

Round 2: Stays MAJOR, strengthened. At least three other design choices feed the loop.

3
Major
The hidden deadline is really an invisible mistake budget, and the player can't read it.

With one fixed speed and an authored route length, a flawless run always takes the same time, so the deadline is a count of allowed collisions. The ship's lights can't tell the player "I can afford two more hits." The outpost search and the final repair spend the budget without testing any skill.

Round 2: Unchanged in severity.

4
Major
Pacing is front-loaded with untimed repairs that don't feed the crossings.

About 20 of the 30 minutes come before boarding, with no pressure. The later repairs have no stated effect on the crossings, and "unlocked routes" has nothing to connect to because the crossings offer no route choice.

Round 2: Stays MAJOR. Recommendation revised: repairs should unlock return route options.

5
MajorR1 Minor → R2 Major
The crossing penalty system lists outcomes without rules, and one penalty has no input.

Water, slowdown, or capsize, with no rule for which applies when. Water has no drain and no threshold. "Cargo mishandling" can't be caused by anything the player does, since their only boat control is steering.

Round 2: Upgraded, because three colleagues depend on the penalty table.

Round 2 cross-examination

Conflicts

  • vs adversarial-qa #1 — Rejects "let the ship keep going", because it creates a doomed run the player can't read. Expiry must resolve at expiry: audio-only where the ship isn't in view, or check the outcome once at the final repair, with pace milestones.
  • vs feasibility-lead #5 — Deferring capsize and cargo only works if the penalty table, with a time cost per event, is written now, so the deadline carries a known reserve.
  • vs narrative-critic #4.2 — Play the fail outcome in full, then offer a retry from a phase-start snapshot. The consequence must not require erasing the whole run.

Connections

  • player-psychologist #3 — A novice's learning collisions feed the death spiral from the first minute of steering. A sheltered harbour stretch fixes both problems.
  • player-psychologist #2 — The curious player pays twice at the outpost: once in deadline and once in difficulty on the return.
  • player-psychologist #5 — A route choice gives repairs an output, the dead schema field a purpose, the deadline a second variable, and the game replay value. It needs the forecast designed for branches from the start.
  • adversarial-qa #3 — A respawn facing a rock at fixed speed chains collisions into the spiral. The grace period should be a rule.
  • feasibility-lead #4 — Par time can be computed on paper. Only the mistake allowance needs a playtest.
  • adversarial-qa #5 — At full-game scale the ship sits frozen on the horizon for roughly 20 minutes.

narrative-critic

Story logic, character motivation, and tone consistency.

Round 1 findings

1
Blocking
Ted is the narrative spine, but his presence is still an open decision, and he disappears after Step 2.

Ted guides the whole opening and is Pillar 3's answer to "no markers", yet "Ted's presence" is unsettled. He is never mentioned in Steps 6–8, and the document never explains why an experienced keeper sends a new apprentice into a lethal storm.

Round 2: Kept BLOCKING. The point that he has no arc after Step 2 is this reviewer's alone.

2
Major
Jeff's motivation and knowledge are unexplained, which undercuts the companion relationship.

The document never says why a just-shipwrecked man goes straight back out, what his reluctance is about, or how a stranger knows the outpost's waters. The rowboat itself is unexplained, so the crash reads as a device for delivering a navigator.

3
Major
The folk-horror layer has no link to the plot, so "dread you can read" has nothing to read.

Every source of dread is scenery. The document never says why the beacon fails, why the outpost is abandoned, or why a spare burner sits there. The prototype removes the folk-horror layer entirely, and no private canon is given for the ambiguity to hide.

Round 2: Kept MAJOR, strengthened.

4
BlockingR1 Major → R2 Blocking
The "consequential ending" has an unnamed victim, an undecided aftermath, and no payoff for the cast.

No character or relationship links anyone to the passenger ship. The retry model is undecided, and an instant checkpoint retry would play as a game-over screen. Ted, Jeff and Daniel never react to either outcome.

Round 2: Upgraded, citing player-psychologist #4 and adversarial-qa #1. The "sails calmly into port" note was withdrawn. The Daniel point was revised: give him a causal role or defer him.

5
Minor
The apprentice's one moral beat is scripted by someone else.

The pitch frames the gate as the apprentice's act, but Ted tells the player to open it and it's a single interaction. The player never decides anything or meets the survivors.

Round 2: Kept MINOR. The gate scene is a natural place for the ship-introduction beat.

Round 2 cross-examination

Conflicts

  • vs business-analyst #5 — Keep the crash: it's the only on-screen preview of the fail-state shipwreck. Daniel should get a role (for example, he's hurt, which is Jeff's motive) or be deferred.
  • vs systems-designer #2 — Position-only weather breaks Pillar 2. Split the channel: the readable storm follows elapsed time, and hazard density follows phase and position.
  • vs adversarial-qa #5 — The ship should first appear at the end of the pre-boarding act, as the reason Ted gives the errand, so the frozen window never exists.

Connections

  • player-psychologist #2 — The folk-horror sits exactly where the clock punishes reading it, which is a theme failure and not only a UX one.
  • adversarial-qa #1 — The ending is undefined in both meaning and presentation. An audio-only fail is strong if it's designed on purpose.
  • player-psychologist #1 — Ted and Jeff outcome lines that refer to the player's actual delays fix legibility and the cast payoff at once.
  • systems-designer #3 — Having Ted at the lamp gives the flat climax weight without adding a skill test.
  • feasibility-lead #2 — Recording voices before a character bible exists is where rework will happen.
  • business-analyst #1, #2 — A coherent hidden canon is the only replay reason the design could have.

player-psychologist

What a first-time player experiences, minute by minute, across a ~30-minute session.

Round 1 findings

1
Blocking
Failing late is undefined, and the player can't tell why they failed.

The fail comes at minutes 20–30 with no decided retry. With a hidden deadline and one lumped penalty, the player can't tell whether collisions, the outpost or the climb cost them. Pillar 4's promise that the player "always knows ... why they failed" can't be kept.

Round 2: Stays BLOCKING, strengthened.

2
Major
The invisible clock quietly punishes curiosity at the outpost.

The folk-horror payoff lives at the outpost, where the hidden clock is running. Players who read closely get the bad ending, and players who rush never see the lore.

Round 2: Stays MAJOR. The "move discoveries pre-boarding" fix was withdrawn in favour of budgeted exploration time with a diegetic cue from Jeff.

3
Major
The player first touches the boat after the clock has started.

Nothing before boarding teaches steering, the "central player skill". A novice's first collisions are silently counted against the ending, while they're also reading hazards and parsing Jeff's directions.

Round 2: Stays MAJOR, strengthened. Close to blocking for first-time players.

4
BlockingR1 Major → R2 Blocking
The stakes are never introduced to the player in-game.

The sequence never shows the moment the player learns the passenger ship exists. The document also doesn't say whether the ship is visible from the outpost, during pickup, or on the climb, all of which happen while the timer is running.

Round 2: Upgraded. Four other reviews assume the player knows about the ship and can read it.

5
Minor
The player can't do anything about the pressure.

With a fixed speed and no throttle, the storm says hurry but the player has no way to hurry. The return crossing may feel like "the same, but harder".

Round 2: Stays MINOR, partly absorbed by systems-designer #3. The playtest check should move earlier than week 6.

Round 2 cross-examination

Conflicts

  • vs adversarial-qa #5 — Hiding the ship leaves no known stakes. Reveal it at the dock just before boarding, which satisfies both requirements.
  • vs business-analyst #5 — Keep the crash, because it shows that boats die on these rocks tonight. Rewrite it to foreshadow the ship rather than cutting it.
  • vs systems-designer #4 — Concedes that moving discoveries before boarding worsens the imbalance. But an untimed front half isn't automatically a flaw; the ratio is the question.
  • vs feasibility-lead #5 — Deferring capsize means Gate 1 measures a friendlier game. The document should say the fairness verdict doesn't cover capsize.

Connections

  • systems-designer #2 — For a first-time player, the spiral starts before any skill exists. The grace stretch and position-only escalation should be treated as one fix.
  • adversarial-qa #1 — The player may not realise they've failed at all. Fail presentation and fail explanation need a single answer.
  • systems-designer #3 — Milestones only work with guaranteed sightlines to the ship, or audio.
  • adversarial-qa #5 — The journal is either an exploit (if it pauses the clock) or a trap (if it doesn't). A diegetic journal that pauses must be chosen deliberately.
  • narrative-critic #1 — Ted is the onboarding. With no second-half Ted, the post-mortem falls to Jeff, who has no defined voice.
  • feasibility-lead #4 — 3–5 testers in the bug-fix week can't judge helplessness versus tension.

feasibility-lead

Scope, timeline and technical buildability: solo developer, Godot 4 (proposed), PC/Steam, 2026 tools.

Round 1 findings

1
Major
The six-week schedule never builds the shared hazard forecast, and week 4 carries too much.

Jeff's calls depend on a forecast API that no week builds. Week 4 bundles five scripted features with its first version, Gate 0 tests steering without Jeff, and the only buffer (week 5) is reserved for waves.

2
Major
The full game has no schedule, estimate, or cut list beyond waves.

Gates 2–3 have no dates, durations or staffing. Voice recording has no source, budget or licensing note, and there's no asset estimate for the final art. There's nothing to measure the slice against.

3
Major
Open decisions block work that is already scheduled.

The engine, Ted's presence, the fail-state follow-up and the target hardware all sit on the Gate 1 critical path while still undecided. The save and deadline contract depends on checkpoints, which are scheduled two gates later.

Round 2: Sharpened, kept MAJOR. The fail-state decision is needed before week 5, and Ted's presence by week 3.

4
Major
Deadline tuning and the 30-minute target can only be checked at the very end.

The deadline is built in week 5 and tuned from 3–5 testers in week 6, the same week as bug fixing. The 30-minute target is only testable at Gate 3.

Round 2: Strengthened by the death spiral and the outpost's future growth.

5
MajorR1 Minor → R2 Major
The prototype's failure and transition policies hide unestimated, critical-path work.

A first-person capsize and recovery with Jeff and the burner aboard, under the no-cutaway rule, is unestimated. The cargo-mishandling deliverable has no spec.

Round 2: Upgraded and retitled. Docking, burner retrieval, respawn orientation and per-phase fail presentations are all on the critical path. Waves should be cut before work starts.

Round 2 cross-examination

Conflicts

  • vs systems-designer #1, adversarial-qa #2 — MAJOR for the prototype, since "fail, then restart the slice" is valid, and BLOCKING for Gate 2. Supports the run-start snapshot fix.
  • vs narrative-critic #1 — Below BLOCKING, because it's a decision, not a build problem. Added Ted beats are unpriced voice-over, so any second-half lines should come from the lighthouse only.
  • vs player-psychologist #5, systems-designer #4 — No route branching in the prototype. Cut the unused "unlocked routes" field instead.
  • vs player-psychologist #3 — Specify the harbour-mouth trigger rather than new level geometry.

Connections

  • adversarial-qa #1 — The fail becomes up to four authored presentations, while week 5 budgets for one. The cheapest version is audio-only, then a lighthouse view.
  • adversarial-qa #3 — Docking, getting off the boat, pickup and respawn are the phase transitions the state contract depends on, not polish.
  • systems-designer #2 — The loop makes deadline sensitivity nonlinear. Position-only escalation can be tuned offline.
  • player-psychologist #2, systems-designer #3 — Prototype outpost durations become invalid once exploration grows at Gate 2.
  • business-analyst #2, narrative-critic #3 — One dressed room on decided hardware would test atmosphere and performance at once.
  • business-analyst #5 — Removing Daniel frees week 4 for the forecast work.

adversarial-qa

Contradictions, unhandled edge cases, sequence breaks, exploits, and unplanned fail states.

Round 1 findings

1
Blocking
The fail state can't be shown from wherever the deadline actually expires.

Expiry can happen mid-crossing, in the outpost, or on the stairs. But the fail is "seen in first person" from the lighthouse, and cutaways are banned. There's no legal way to show it.

2
Blocking
Checkpoints that save remaining time can create unwinnable saves, and the retry model contradicts "resolves exactly once".

A low-time checkpoint creates a doomed loop and breaks "no dead-end save". A checkpoint retry resolves the same save slot twice, and nothing says whether the ship's state rewinds.

Round 2: Severity stays; scope widened to cover weather state on reload.

3
Major
A boat with one fixed speed has no defined way to stop, dock, disembark, or reach a recovery point.

Arrival triggers, arriving home without the burner, retrieving a burner at "a marked nearby point", and respawn orientation are all unspecified.

Round 2: Stays MAJOR, reframed as a missing "boat stopped or boarding" spec with four consumers.

4
Major
"Proposed" items are enforced as invariants and gate criteria, and Ted is still an open decision.

Proposed rules (the deadline start, burner recoverability, the crash trigger) already appear as hard invariants and gate checks. Every early gate relies on Ted.

Round 2: The Ted half softened. The Proposed-as-invariant half kept at MAJOR.

5
Minor
Deadline pause and preset rules open exploits, and the pre-boarding state is frozen and ambiguous.

Four small gaps: whether the journal pauses the clock, swapping the difficulty preset mid-run, a ship frozen before boarding, and whether the gate can be used before the crash.

Round 2: The frozen-ship bullet becomes MAJOR if player-psychologist #4 is adopted. The other bullets stay MINOR.

Round 2 cross-examination

Conflicts

  • vs feasibility-lead (overall) — A Gate 1 criterion that depends on a Gate 3 system means the prototype can't pass its own gate. That is BLOCKING.
  • vs player-psychologist #4 — A ship Ted points out must hold still through the whole untimed act. The document must say what it does before boarding.
  • vs player-psychologist #5 — A shortcut breaks the singular safe-route signal, is always optimal at fixed speed, and doubles the tuning load.
  • vs narrative-critic #1 — Kept at MAJOR. Silent canonisation of Proposed rules is worse than a flagged open question.

Connections

  • systems-designer #2 — An unwinnable save becomes the normal outcome for struggling players. The save contract must list weather intensity.
  • systems-designer #1 — "First boarding" implies a get-off and re-board cycle that isn't defined. Jeff should refuse to cast off without the burner.
  • systems-designer #5, feasibility-lead #5 — Write one spec for "the boat is not moving". If capsize is cut, cut water too.
  • narrative-critic #5 — A real gate choice can be refused, which needs a rule.
  • player-psychologist #2 — Curious players are in the one place with no sightline to the ship, which makes #1 the common case.
