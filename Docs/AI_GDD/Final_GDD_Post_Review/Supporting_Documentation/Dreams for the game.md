Lighting the Storm — Dreams for the Game

Status: ideas outside the prototype and full-game scope, 25 September 2026, updated 1 October 2026 (added “More outpost detail”; Daniel’s reason for being shaken updated after the crash was removed). Nothing here is canon or scheduled. Build an item only after the creator approves it, and only if it still fits the four design pillars and the scope rule in the Executive Summary.

Companion choice

Before boarding, the player chooses Jeff or Daniel to cross with. Either companion can complete both crossings, and the choice changes how guidance is delivered rather than whether the route can be finished.

• Needs: a choice scene at the lighthouse, a companion ID in saved state, and dialogue and cues for both companions.
• Testing: every phase and both ship outcomes must be completable with each companion.

Daniel as a companion

In the current design Daniel is shaken by the storm and shelters in the basement. If the companion choice is built, he becomes the alternative crossing companion with a different guidance style:

• Characterisation: a younger steersman, defensive about the wreck. He blames the crash on the lighthouse’s weak, flickering beacon — a real problem, but not the whole story, since he was also sailing recklessly in rough water. Admitting that would make him responsible, so he leans on the beacon’s failure: a useful but unreliable witness. In the choice version, he is the one who names the passenger ship the moment you board, and Jeff is visibly relieved when the player picks him over Daniel, whom he considers reckless.
• Guidance: he points a flashlight toward the way to go. It reads faster than Jeff’s verbal calls, but it is occasionally wrong.
• Rules: his light reads from the same safe-route signal as the waves and boat, with a difficulty-dependent error rate. His mistakes must be learnable and recoverable, and must always be catchable from the visible waves; never make both the world and the signal unreadable at once.
• Accessibility: the flashlight direction must be readable without relying only on colour, with a subtitle or visual direction option.
• Tension to resolve: deliberately wrong guidance works against pillar 4 (dread you can read: the player always knows what to do), so it needs playtesting before it is committed.

Hidden coda

An ending unlocked by piecing together journal fragments, shrines, and symbols across the lighthouse and outpost, revealing what happened to whoever kept the lighthouse before Ted.

• Tension to resolve: the source GDD promises to reveal what the symbols “actually mean”, which conflicts with the rule that the supernatural stays unconfirmed. Proposed: reveal historical relationships or events while leaving the supernatural explanation open.

More outpost detail

Until the prototype is considered complete, the outpost stays minimal and no significant detail is added to it. After that, it could carry more folk-horror atmosphere, canon clues and in-world documentation: additional log fragments, shrines, symbols, notes and subtly strange architecture that suggest the history of the island and the earlier keepers without settling its cause.

• Needs: additional authored props and documents, journal entries, optional discovery interactions, and audio and lighting passes, all as data with stable IDs.
• Constraint: it must keep to the scope rule (no new major location) and stay unexplained, so the supernatural is never confirmed. The outpost is untimed, so any discovery here should not push pacing against the 30-minute target.
• Link: this feeds the Hidden coda below if that is built.

Full Gale mode

An optional New Game+ modifier that remixes the parts-finding and boat-crossing steps into a tougher, repeatable run for players who want navigation to be a real skill test.

• Needs: harder wave presets, remixed routes or part locations, and a deadline tuned for experienced players.
• Constraint: it must stay legible; harder should mean more demanding waves, not less readable ones.
