Lighting the Storm — Decisions to be Made

Status: open questions for the creator, 25 September 2026. Each item lists what is unresolved and any Proposed answer. When a decision is made, update the Executive Summary or Technical Strategy and remove it from this list.

Story and canon

1. Ted’s presence. The source GDD describes both live barked directions and a keeper heard only in recordings. The current brief already assumes a live Ted, who guides the repairs, prompts the player to look out of the window, and tells them to open the gate. Proposed: Ted is present and speaks live; recordings and journals belong to earlier keepers. Confirm before recording dialogue.
2. Daniel after the gate. Daniel stays at the lighthouse while the player and Jeff cross. Decide whether he has any later role, such as dialogue on return or presence at the outcome, or simply stays out of sight.

Ship deadline and fail state

3. Deadline length. Set from measured playtests so a first-time player who follows cues has room for some mistakes. Needs a first value for the prototype.
4. Pause and checkpoints. Proposed: the deadline stops during menus and deliberate pause, keeps running through crossings, recoveries, the pickup and the final repair, and remaining time is saved at checkpoints. Confirm, and decide where the checkpoints are.
5. After the fail state. When the ship runs aground, decide what happens next: restart from the last checkpoint, restart the crossing, restart the whole run, or end on the fail outcome with a retry option.

Boat and crossings

6. Crossing failure policy. Proposed: a collision with a rock or debris (or a broadside wave hit, if the wave stretch goal is built) adds water, slows the boat or causes a brief capsize and recovery at a nearby safe point, all costing deadline time. The burner can never be permanently lost; it stays recoverable aboard or at a marked nearby point. Confirm.
7. Bailing interaction. Add a simple bailing action only if playtests show it improves the crossing without overloading the one-control steering promise.

Production

8. Engine. Proposed: Godot 4 with GDScript for the first playable. Confirm, or switch to Unity if an existing project or experience favours it.
9. Target hardware and the Gate 0 feel target. Define the target PC and what “enjoyable steering” means well enough to judge the steering spike.
10. Controller support in the prototype. Technical Strategy’s Gate 1 requires keyboard and controller; the Executive Summary lists controller support under the full game. Decide whether the prototype needs it.
11. Prototype repair. Choose which single lighthouse repair appears in the prototype, and the full repair list for the full game.

Stretch goals

12. Wave hazards. Waves that push or roll the boat are a stretch goal (Executive Summary, Technical Strategy). Decide at the end of week 4 whether the schedule has room to start them in week 5, and after playtests whether they stay in the full game.
